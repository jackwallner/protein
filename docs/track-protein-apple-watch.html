How to Track Protein on Apple Watch, Without Scanning Food

By Jack Wallner. Updated October 12, 2026.

To track protein on Apple Watch, use an app that logs grams from the wrist and writes them to Apple Health, then put its complication on your watch face. Set a daily gram target, save the amounts you eat repeatedly as one-tap buttons, and read protein off the Nutrition Facts label instead of estimating. Reference points for a target are 0.8 g per kilogram of body weight per day as a minimum, and 1.4 to 2.0 g per kilogram for people who exercise.

Why the watch needs help from an app

Apple stores protein in the Health app as a data type called Dietary Protein, which Apple's developer documentation defines as the amount of protein consumed. Health holds what you or an app puts into it, so tracking protein on your wrist comes down to choosing the app that does the logging and the glance. Three setups work, and they trade speed against detail.

Set it up in five steps

  1. Get a daily gram target. If a clinician or dietitian gave you one, use theirs. Otherwise see the reference points below.
  2. Pick the foods you repeat. Most people eat the same shake, eggs or yogurt most days. Read the grams from the Nutrition Facts label, which lists protein in grams per serving, and save those amounts as one-tap buttons.
  3. Put the total on your watch face. A complication shows the running gram total at a glance, so you see it without opening anything.
  4. Log when you eat. A quick-add button covers the usual foods and a Digital Crown dial covers everything else.
  5. Check the sources. If two apps both write protein to Health for the same meal, it can be counted twice. Keep one logger per meal.

Reference points for a daily number

These are general reference points, not advice for any one person.

  • 0.8 g per kilogram of body weight per day is the minimum recommended for adults, from the National Academy of Medicine as summarized by Harvard's Nutrition Source. For an 80 kg (176 lb) adult that is 64 g.
  • 1.4 to 2.0 g per kilogram per day is the range the International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand calls sufficient for most exercising individuals. For the same 80 kg adult that is 112 to 160 g.

People on medication, recovering from surgery or managing a condition often have a clinician-set number that overrides both. Use that number if you have one.

Why grams beat guessing

Protein is a number you can read off a label or look up once. A saved 30 g shake logs the same 30 g every day. Estimating from a photo of a plate can return a different figure for the same meal, and you then have to check it. For a single-number goal, set amounts you chose yourself are easier to trust.

Frequently asked questions

Can you track protein on Apple Watch?

Yes. Install an app that logs protein from the wrist and writes it to Apple Health, then add its complication to your watch face. A grams-only app lets you log a usual shake with one tap.

Does Apple Health track protein?

Health has a Dietary Protein data type that stores grams of protein consumed. It fills from what you or a connected app add, so you need an app to do the logging.

How much protein do I need per day?

A common minimum for adults is 0.8 g per kilogram of body weight per day, and the International Society of Sports Nutrition cites 1.4 to 2.0 g per kilogram per day as sufficient for most exercising people. If a clinician gave you a target, follow theirs.

Do I have to count calories to track protein?

No. Protein is listed in grams on the Nutrition Facts label, so you can track grams alone and ignore calories.

Can two apps log protein to Apple Health at the same time?

They can, but a meal logged in both may be counted twice. Choose one logger per meal and check which apps are writing protein.
